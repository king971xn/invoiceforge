5 Invoice Templates Every Small Business Should Have

Different types of business transactions call for different invoice formats. Here are the 5 templates that cover virtually every billing scenario a small business encounters — and how to use each one effectively.

1. The Service Invoice

This is your bread-and-butter template. Use it for consulting, design, development, writing, coaching — any service-based work. It should include:

  • Hourly rate or project fee
  • Detailed description of services rendered
  • Service period dates
  • Quantity of hours or units

Best for: Freelancers, consultants, agencies, coaches, and any service provider billing by the hour or by project.

2. The Product Invoice

If you sell physical or digital products, this template is what you need. Unlike service invoices, product invoices include:

  • SKU or product codes
  • Quantity shipped
  • Unit price and line total
  • Shipping charges (as a separate line item)
  • Applicable sales tax

Best for: E-commerce sellers, wholesalers, manufacturers, and digital product creators.

3. The Recurring / Retainer Invoice

For clients on a monthly retainer or subscription, a recurring invoice template saves you from recreating the same bill every month. Key elements:

  • Fixed monthly fee
  • Service description (same each month)
  • Billing period (e.g., "June 1-30, 2026")
  • Automatic numbering for tracking

Best for: Retainer clients, subscription businesses, maintenance contracts, and ongoing support agreements.

4. The Proforma Invoice

A proforma invoice is sent before work begins — it's essentially a quote formatted as a draft invoice. It helps clients approve costs upfront and is often required for international transactions. Mark it clearly as "Proforma — Not a Tax Invoice" to avoid confusion.

Best for: New client proposals, international orders, large projects requiring upfront approval, and customs documentation.

5. The Credit Note / Refund Invoice

When you need to issue a refund or correct an overcharge, a credit note is the proper document. It should reference the original invoice number and clearly state the credited amount as a negative value or separate credit line.

Best for: Refunds, discounts applied after invoicing, billing corrections, and returned products.
